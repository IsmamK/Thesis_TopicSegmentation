\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary of findings}

This thesis presented \lecseg{}, a multimodal, hierarchical lecture-video
topic segmentation system, and the \dataset{} benchmark of 30 annotated
academic videos.
The proposed reliability-weighted fusion (N2) successfully down-weights
uninformative modalities without labelled data, producing a measurable
$P_k$ reduction over fixed-weight baselines (RQ1).
The two-stage boundary predictor (N1) improved precision--recall balance
over single-threshold methods by separating candidate generation from
refinement (RQ2).
Explicit hierarchical modelling (N3) with the nesting constraint produced
the lowest hierarchical WindowDiff scores, confirming that joint chapter and
subtopic prediction outperforms two independent flat passes (RQ3).
Local-LLM boundary refinement (N4) had a modest effect on boundary metrics
but meaningfully improved title quality in manual evaluation (RQ4).
All gains of the full \lecseg{}-Hierarchical system over the best baseline
(BertSeg) were statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.05$ after Holm
correction (RQ5).

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Corpus size.}
    \dataset{} contains 30 videos.
    While this is comparable to prior benchmarks~\citep{choi2000latent},
    it is too small for training a neural model from scratch and may not
    generalise to languages other than English or lecture styles outside the
    five domains studied.

  \item \textbf{Silver chapter labels.}
    Creator-supplied YouTube chapters serve as chapter-level ground truth.
    These labels are not pedagogically vetted and may reflect production
    choices (e.g., chapter breaks tied to sponsor reads) rather than topic
    transitions.

  \item \textbf{LLM title quality variability.}
    Llama-3.1-8B occasionally generates titles that paraphrase an isolated
    example rather than the segment's main concept, particularly for
    highly mathematical content.
    Prompt engineering or model fine-tuning could mitigate this.

  \item \textbf{No streaming variant.}
    The pipeline processes a complete video offline.
    Real-time or low-latency segmentation for live lectures is not addressed.

  \item \textbf{Prosody and OCR not fully integrated.}
    Shot boundaries, slide OCR text, and prosodic features were extracted
    (Chapters~\ref{chap:methodology} and~\ref{chap:results}) but prosody
    was not included in the final RWF fusion due to time constraints.
    Preliminary experiments showed a small additional $P_k$ gain; this is
    deferred to future work.
\end{itemize}

\section{Closing remarks}

The explosion of online educational content has created both an opportunity
and a responsibility: with the right tools, the hours of expert knowledge
captured in lecture recordings can become as searchable and navigable as a
well-indexed textbook.
\lecseg{} demonstrates that state-of-the-art topic segmentation is achievable
with entirely open-source components, running on commodity hardware, without
any labelled training data beyond the benchmark itself.
We hope the \dataset{} corpus and the \lecseg{} codebase will serve as a
foundation for future work on lecture understanding, adaptive learning systems,
and multimodal educational retrieval.
