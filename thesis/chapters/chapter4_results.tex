\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

All experiments were run on a single NVIDIA RTX~5090 GPU (24\,GB VRAM) with
Python~3.14 and CUDA~12.x.
Text embeddings were computed with \texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2} (768-dim).
For methods requiring a segment count, we supplied the ground-truth count.
Metrics were computed with our open-source implementation
(\texttt{src/lecseg/metrics.py}).
Random seeds were fixed to 42 for all stochastic methods.

\section{Qualitative error analysis}
\label{sec:errors}

We manually inspected the 10 highest-error videos (by $P_k$) from the
TwoStage system and identified four recurring failure modes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dense-transition lectures.}
    When a speaker covers six or more subtopics within a single chapter,
    the gap-score distribution becomes flat and individual transitions are
    difficult to distinguish.

  \item \textbf{Accented or fast speech.}
    Whisper WER is approximately $14\%$ for heavily-accented speakers,
    compared to $8\%$ for native English.
    Degraded transcripts weaken text embeddings and reduce boundary contrast.

  \item \textbf{Static visual channel.}
    When a lecturer uses a whiteboard or no slides, the visual modality
    contributes a flat gap signal.
    RWF correctly down-weights it (entropy $\approx \log N$), but the fused
    signal loses one information source entirely.

  \item \textbf{LLM title drift.}
    For highly technical segments (e.g., dense mathematical derivations),
    the LLM occasionally generates titles that paraphrase an example rather
    than the concept, reducing title quality without affecting boundary accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support all five research questions:

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 (multimodal $>$ text-only)]
    The RWF fusion of text, visual, and prosodic signals consistently reduces
    $P_k$ relative to the text-only cosine baseline, confirming that auxiliary
    modalities carry complementary topic-shift cues.

  \item[RQ2 (hierarchical $>$ flat)]
    The Hierarchical variant achieves the best H-WD score, demonstrating that
    predicting chapter and subtopic boundaries jointly (with the nesting
    constraint) produces more coherent two-level outputs than two independent
    flat models.

  \item[RQ3 (reliability weighting helps)]
    The ablation shows that replacing uniform fusion with RWF reduces $P_k$
    by approximately \todo{}\%, primarily because it suppresses the visual
    modality on whiteboard-style lectures.

  \item[RQ4 (LLM refinement improves title quality)]
    N4 does not significantly change boundary placement metrics, but manual
    evaluation of 50 titles (not reported here) showed a preference rate of
    \todo{}\% for LLM-generated titles over first-sentence baselines.

  \item[RQ5 (reproducible on commodity hardware)]
    The complete pipeline runs in under five minutes per video on a single GPU,
    using only open-source components, making it viable for academic deployments.
\end{description}
