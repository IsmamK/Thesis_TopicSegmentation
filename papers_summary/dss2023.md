## Problem (2 sentences)
Academic video understanding—including temporal topic segmentation—lacks a large-scale, diverse, publicly available benchmark derived from real university lectures. AVLectures addresses this by introducing a dataset of over 2,350 lectures from MIT OpenCourseWare and a baseline segmentation system based on unsupervised clustering of multimodal features.

## Method (5 bullets)
- Collects 86 MIT-OCW courses comprising 2,350+ lecture videos (~2,200 hours total), with existing chapter annotations from OCW used as ground-truth segment boundaries.
- Extracts multimodal representations for each video: visual frame features (ResNet/ViT), OCR text from slide frames, and ASR transcripts, aligned to a common temporal grid.
- Applies TW-FINCH (Time-Window Fast Incremental Nearest-neighbour Clustering for Humans), an unsupervised clustering algorithm, to group temporally adjacent feature vectors into coherent segments.
- Evaluates on 15 annotated courses from the full collection, comparing the clustering baseline against several text-only and visual-only ablations.
- Releases the dataset, annotations, and code publicly to enable reproducible benchmarking.

## Limitations (3 bullets, from the paper itself)
- The segmentation baseline is unsupervised and produces flat (single-level) boundaries without subtopic structure.
- Ground-truth boundaries are derived from OCW chapter metadata provided by instructors, which may be coarser or less consistent than expert-annotated boundaries.
- The dataset covers only English MIT lectures, limiting generalisability to other languages and teaching styles.

## How it relates to our work (1 paragraph)
AVLectures is the most closely related prior work to LECSEG and is cited prominently in Chapter 2 and Chapter 3. LECSEG's NOVELTY_TRACKER identifies `dss2023_avlectures` as the primary gap motivating N1 (the baseline is flat and only partially multimodal), N3 (no subtopic level), and N5 (single-annotator boundaries without inter-annotator κ). LECSEG's LECSEG-30 dataset is smaller but has dual-annotator κ-validated hierarchical boundaries and a public DOI release.

## Differences from our approach (tied to novelty claims)
- **N1** (hierarchical multimodal): AVLectures baseline is unsupervised and single-level; LECSEG is a supervised hierarchical system with a learned two-stage boundary predictor.
- **N2** (reliability-weighted fusion): AVLectures concatenates modalities without learned reliability weighting; LECSEG uses learned gating.
- **N3** (two-level output): AVLectures produces flat chapter-level boundaries only; LECSEG produces chapter + subtopic jointly.
- **N4** (local-LLM refinement): No LLM refinement; LECSEG uses local Llama 3.1 for boundary verification and auto-titling.
- **N5** (LECSEG-30 dataset): OCW chapter metadata is single-annotator and not κ-validated; LECSEG-30 has dual-annotator κ and hierarchical labels.
- **N6** (5-metric eval + CIs): AVLectures reports a limited metric set without bootstrap CIs; LECSEG reports five metrics with CIs and Wilcoxon tests.
- **N7** (reproducibility): AVLectures releases data and code; LECSEG additionally pins seeds, environment, and ships `make reproduce` for full table/figure regeneration.
