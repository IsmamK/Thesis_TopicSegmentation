## Problem (2 sentences)
Learning general-purpose temporal video representations from unlabelled video requires scalable self-supervised objectives that capture both visual appearance and temporal structure. This paper introduces the Contrastive Bidirectional Transformer (CBT), which combines BERT-style masked prediction with a noise-contrastive estimation loss over sequences of video clip features.

## Method (5 bullets)
- Pre-processes videos into short clip-level feature vectors using a frozen visual backbone (S3D or similar), producing a sequence of clip representations analogous to BERT's token sequence.
- Applies a bidirectional Transformer encoder to the clip sequence and trains with a Masked Token Prediction objective: randomly masking clip features and predicting them from context using noise-contrastive estimation (NCE) rather than cross-entropy over a vocabulary.
- Combines the visual NCE loss with an optional cross-modal alignment loss using ASR word sequences, aligning spoken language to visual clip representations.
- Pre-trains on large-scale video datasets (Kinetics-600, HowTo100M) without any manual labels; representations are then transferred by fine-tuning on downstream tasks.
- Evaluates on action recognition (UCF101, HMDB51), video captioning (YouCook2), and temporal action segmentation (COIN).

## Datasets used

| Dataset | Size | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetics-600 (pre-training) | ~500K clips | General video |
| HowTo100M (pre-training) | ~1.22M instructional videos | Instructional video |
| UCF101 (eval) | 13,320 clips, 101 classes | Action recognition |
| HMDB51 (eval) | 7,000 clips, 51 classes | Action recognition |
| YouCook2 (eval) | 2,000 instructional videos | Captioning |
| COIN (eval) | 11,827 videos | Action segmentation |

## Metrics & headline results

| Metric | Value | Dataset |
|---|---|---|
| Top-1 accuracy | 79.5% | UCF101 (fine-tuned) |
| Top-1 accuracy | 44.5% | HMDB51 (fine-tuned) |
| BLEU-4 | 5.12 | YouCook2 |
| Action segmentation accuracy | 53.9% | COIN |

## Limitations (3 bullets, from the paper itself)
- Pre-training requires large-scale video datasets (Kinetics, HowTo100M) that are expensive to collect and process; the approach is not accessible without significant compute.
- The method is evaluated on clip-level action recognition and captioning; direct application to lecture-style topic segmentation with hierarchical boundaries is not demonstrated.
- The contrastive objective requires careful design of negative samples; poor negatives can degrade learned representations.

## How it relates to our work (1 paragraph)
Sun et al. 2019 demonstrates that bidirectional Transformers trained with contrastive objectives produce strong temporal video representations, which informs LECSEG's choice of a bidirectional encoder for the boundary predictor (N1). LECSEG does not use CBT directly, but the architectural insight—that a bidirectional encoder with a global loss outperforms frame-by-frame classifiers—motivates the two-stage boundary predictor design. CBT is cited in LECSEG Chapter 2 under related multimodal representation learning.

## Differences from our approach (tied to novelty claims)
- **N1** (hierarchical multimodal): CBT is a general pre-training method for action recognition/captioning; LECSEG is a specialised lecture segmentation pipeline with a hierarchical output head.
- **N2** (reliability-weighted fusion): CBT does not gate modalities by reliability; LECSEG adds learned reliability weighting.
- **N3** (two-level output): CBT has no hierarchical output; LECSEG produces chapter + subtopic.
- **N4** (local-LLM refinement): No LLM refinement in CBT; LECSEG adds a local-LLM verification step.
- **N5** (LECSEG-30 dataset): CBT evaluated on general video benchmarks; LECSEG uses a lecture-specific corpus.
- **N6** (5-metric eval + CIs): CBT reports accuracy/BLEU; LECSEG uses five segmentation metrics with bootstrap CIs.
- **N7** (reproducibility): CBT releases pre-trained model weights; LECSEG additionally ships configs, seeds, and `make reproduce`.
