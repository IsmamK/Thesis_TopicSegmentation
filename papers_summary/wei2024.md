## Problem (2 sentences)
Pre-class video-based learning requires students to understand instructional video content without instructor guidance, yet existing video understanding tools do not provide structured segmentation and question-answering grounded in lecture-specific content. PreMind proposes a multi-agent vision-language model (VLM) pipeline that segments instructional videos by slides, transcribes and corrects ASR output, and supports content-grounded QA.

## Method (5 bullets)
- Uses PySceneDetect combined with a vision-language model (VLM) to detect slide transitions and group frames into coherent presentation segments.
- Applies an ASR system to transcribe lecture audio, followed by a VLM-based error-correction agent that fixes domain-specific vocabulary mistakes using slide-text context.
- A visual refinement critic agent iteratively re-examines segment boundaries using the VLM to resolve ambiguous cases and improve alignment between slide content and transcript text.
- Generates segment-level structured summaries and supports question-answering over segmented content using a large closed-API LLM (GPT-4 or similar).
- Evaluates on two internal lecture video datasets (LPM and EI) using segmentation F1 and QA accuracy.

## Limitations (3 bullets, from the paper itself)
- The pipeline depends on closed-source large language models (GPT-4), making it not fully reproducible or cost-free for researchers without API access.
- Evaluation is conducted on two proprietary internal datasets; results cannot be independently verified or compared with public benchmarks.
- The approach is tailored to slide-based instructional videos and may not generalise to chalkboard, lab-demo, or talking-head lecture styles.

## How it relates to our work (1 paragraph)
PreMind is cited in LECSEG Chapter 2 and the NOVELTY_TRACKER as the primary gap motivating N4 and N7. Its reliance on closed GPT-4 makes it non-reproducible and expensive; LECSEG replaces closed-API LLMs with a locally-served open model (Llama 3.1-8B via Ollama) whose prompts and responses are fully cached and publicly released. PreMind also lacks the hierarchical two-level output (N3) and a publicly available evaluated dataset (N5).

## Differences from our approach (tied to novelty claims)
- **N1** (hierarchical multimodal): PreMind produces flat slide-level segments; LECSEG produces hierarchical chapter + subtopic boundaries across four modalities.
- **N2** (reliability-weighted fusion): PreMind does not learn modality reliability weights; its VLM fusion is implicit.
- **N3** (two-level output): No subtopic level; LECSEG outputs two levels jointly.
- **N4** (local-LLM refinement): PreMind uses closed GPT-4, not reproducible; LECSEG uses local open Llama 3.1-8B with full prompt/response caching.
- **N5** (LECSEG-30 dataset): Evaluated on two proprietary, unreleased internal datasets; LECSEG-30 is public with κ-validated labels.
- **N6** (5-metric eval + CIs): PreMind reports F1 and QA accuracy; LECSEG reports five segmentation metrics with bootstrap CIs and Wilcoxon tests.
- **N7** (reproducibility): Closed API dependency breaks reproducibility; LECSEG ships `make reproduce` with pinned dependencies and seeds.
