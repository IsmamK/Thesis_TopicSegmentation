## Problem (2 sentences)

Neural topic segmentation models struggle to capture long-range coherence in lengthy documents, limiting their ability to detect topical boundaries accurately. Existing methods do not explicitly model the structural relationships between sentences or enforce that sentences within the same topic share similar representations.

## Method (5 bullets)
- Build on a Longformer-based encoder pretrained for long-document understanding.
- Propose Topic-aware Sentence Structure Prediction (TSSP): train the model to predict structural relationships between adjacent sentences in documents that have been deliberately shuffled at both topic and sentence levels, forcing it to learn coherence signals.
- Propose Contrastive Semantic Similarity Learning (CSSL): construct contrastive samples using inter- and intra-topic information so that sentences within the same topic have higher representational similarity than those from different topics.
- Combine TSSP and CSSL as auxiliary objectives during fine-tuning to enrich the backbone model's coherence awareness.
- Evaluate in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings to measure generalisability of the learned coherence representations.

## Limitations (3 bullets, from the paper itself)
- The approach relies on supervised training with large annotated corpora (WIKI-727K); performance on smaller or domain-specific datasets is not fully characterised.
- Evaluation focuses on Wikipedia-style text; generalisation to spoken or multimodal content is not examined.
- Specific limitations beyond scope are not reported in the abstract.

## How it relates to our work (1 paragraph)

This paper is directly related as a recent state-of-the-art supervised text-only topic segmentation baseline. Its TSSP and CSSL objectives demonstrate that coherence modelling — enforcing intra-topic similarity and inter-topic separation — is a powerful inductive bias for boundary detection. Our LECSEG pipeline targets lecture videos rather than Wikipedia articles, must handle spoken, noisy transcripts alongside visual slide signals, and produces two-level hierarchical output; this paper provides a strong single-level text-only reference point against which the contribution of multimodal fusion can be measured.

## Differences from our approach (tied to novelty claims)
- **N1** (hierarchical multimodal): This work is text-only and single-level; LECSEG adds slide-change, audio energy, and other visual/acoustic streams in a hierarchical chapter + subtopic architecture.
- **N2** (reliability-weighted fusion): No modality fusion; LECSEG learns gating weights that adapt to per-segment signal quality across modalities.
- **N3** (two-level output): Produces flat segment boundaries; LECSEG outputs both chapter-level and subtopic-level boundaries simultaneously.
- **N4** (local-LLM refinement): No LLM post-processing for boundary adjustment or auto-titling; LECSEG adds a local-LLM refinement stage.
- **N5** (LECSEG-30 dataset): Evaluated on Wikipedia corpora; LECSEG-30 is the first dedicated lecture-video segmentation benchmark with multi-level ground-truth labels.
- **N6** (5-metric eval + CIs): Reports F₁ and P_k only; LECSEG reports five metrics with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **N7** (reproducibility): Not reported in abstract; LECSEG commits to full reproducible release including code, weights, and dataset.
