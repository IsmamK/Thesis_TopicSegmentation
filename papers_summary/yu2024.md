## Problem (2 sentences)
Lecture video topic segmentation requires jointly modelling coherence across text (ASR), visual slide content, and audio, yet most existing approaches use either a single modality or simple feature concatenation that fails to capture cross-modal interactions. This paper proposes a middle-fusion transformer with cross-attention and Mixture-of-Experts variants, combined with multimodal contrastive pre-training, to better model inter-modal coherence for lecture segmentation.

## Method (5 bullets)
- Extracts three modality streams: ASR transcript sentence embeddings, visual keyframe features (slide images via CLIP), and acoustic features, aligned to a shared sentence-level timeline.
- Applies a middle-fusion Transformer architecture with cross-modal attention heads that enable each modality to attend to representations from the other modalities.
- Introduces a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) fusion variant where different experts specialise in different modality combinations, improving generalisation.
- Pre-trains the fusion encoder with a multimodal contrastive objective and kernel density estimation (KDE)-based pseudo-boundary mining to supervise the encoder without manual boundary labels.
- Introduces CLVTS, a new Chinese lecture video topic segmentation dataset with 510 videos (~395 hours), and evaluates on both CLVTS and the AVLectures benchmark.

## Limitations (3 bullets, from the paper itself)
- The fusion architecture uses fixed concatenation of modality embeddings prior to the transformer; it does not learn to downweight unreliable modalities dynamically, which is a gap LECSEG addresses with reliability-weighted gating.
- The CLVTS dataset is in Chinese and the model is not evaluated cross-lingually; generalisation to other languages is not assessed.
- The paper does not provide a publicly reproducible codebase or pinned experimental configuration as of the arXiv submission.

## How it relates to our work (1 paragraph)
Yu et al. 2024 is the most directly comparable prior work to LECSEG in terms of multimodal lecture segmentation and is cited prominently in LECSEG Chapters 2 and 4. The NOVELTY_TRACKER cites `yu2024_multimodal` as gap evidence for N2: their system uses fixed concatenation without reliability gating. LECSEG's reliability-weighted fusion (N2) is directly motivated by this limitation. LECSEG additionally provides hierarchical two-level output (N3), a local-LLM refinement step (N4), and a publicly released κ-validated English benchmark (N5).

## Differences from our approach (tied to novelty claims)
- **N1** (hierarchical multimodal): Yu et al. produce single-level chapter boundaries; LECSEG is hierarchical with chapter + subtopic output.
- **N2** (reliability-weighted fusion): Yu et al. use fixed concatenation or MoE without per-modality reliability estimation; LECSEG learns explicit reliability gates that adapt to source quality (chalkboard vs. slide-based).
- **N3** (two-level output): No subtopic level; LECSEG outputs two levels jointly with constrained decoding.
- **N4** (local-LLM refinement): No LLM refinement or auto-titling; LECSEG adds local Llama 3.1 for boundary verification and chapter titling.
- **N5** (LECSEG-30 dataset): CLVTS is Chinese and not yet publicly released with κ annotations; LECSEG-30 is English, open, and κ-validated.
- **N6** (5-metric eval + CIs): Yu et al. report F1/BS/mIoU without bootstrap CIs; LECSEG reports five metrics with CIs and Wilcoxon tests.
- **N7** (reproducibility): No reproducibility artefact published; LECSEG ships `make reproduce`.
