## Problem (2 sentences)
Recorded classroom lecture videos are difficult to navigate without knowing where topic changes occur. This paper proposes an automatic segmentation approach using text similarity over OCR-extracted slide content and ASR-derived transcripts to detect topic boundaries in classroom recordings.

## Method (5 bullets)
- Extracts text from video frames using OCR to obtain slide content at each sampled keyframe, and obtains ASR transcripts from the audio stream.
- Computes vocabulary overlap (TF-IDF cosine similarity) between consecutive text windows to estimate lexical cohesion across potential segment boundaries.
- Applies a sliding-window approach with a valley-detection heuristic to identify positions where cohesion drops sharply, indicating a topic transition.
- Evaluates the method on 25 classroom videos from the University of Houston, comparing predicted boundaries to instructor-annotated ground truth.
- Reports performance using precision, recall, and F1 at a tolerance of ±30 seconds around annotated boundaries.

## Limitations (3 bullets, from the paper itself)
- The approach relies heavily on slide-projected text; lectures with minimal slide use or chalkboard-only delivery are poorly handled.
- ASR errors propagate into the text similarity computation, degrading performance on low-quality audio recordings.
- The dataset is not publicly released, limiting reproducibility and comparison by other researchers.

## How it relates to our work (1 paragraph)
Tuna et al. 2015 is one of the earliest multimodal approaches to lecture video segmentation and is cited in LECSEG Chapter 2 as a prior-art baseline. Their reliance on OCR text and ASR transcripts is directly comparable to LECSEG's text and OCR channels; however, LECSEG adds visual embeddings, prosodic features, and a learned reliability weighting across modalities. The UH dataset is not publicly available, which motivates LECSEG's release of LECSEG-30 as an open κ-validated benchmark (N5).

## Differences from our approach (tied to novelty claims)
- **N1** (hierarchical multimodal): Tuna et al. produce single-level flat boundaries; LECSEG produces chapter + subtopic hierarchy with four modalities.
- **N2** (reliability-weighted fusion): Tuna et al. heuristically combine OCR and ASR text without learned reliability gating; LECSEG learns per-modality weights.
- **N3** (two-level output): Tuna et al. output one level; LECSEG outputs two.
- **N4** (local-LLM refinement): No LLM refinement or auto-titling.
- **N5** (LECSEG-30 dataset): Tuna et al. use a closed, unpublished dataset; LECSEG releases LECSEG-30 openly with κ annotation.
- **N6** (5-metric eval + CIs): Tuna et al. report F1 only without CIs; LECSEG reports five metrics with bootstrap CIs.
- **N7** (reproducibility): No public code or reproducibility artefact.
